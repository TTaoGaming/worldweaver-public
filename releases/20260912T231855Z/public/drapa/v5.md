## IV. Yoneda — the theorem, the attempted application, the poem

The original words “Yoneda,” “topos,” “fixed point,” “shadow,” and “web” remain in the record. A proper unfolding states the relation between them rather than deleting the words or asserting that they are all the same theorem.

For a locally small category **C**, an object **A**, and a presheaf **F:Cᵒᵖ→Set**, the contravariant Yoneda lemma gives a natural bijection:

```text
Nat(Hom_C(-, A), F) ≅ F(A)

η ↦ η_A(id_A)

x ↦ [f:X→A ↦ F(f)(x)]
```

Appropriate size conditions make the natural-transformation collection a set. Naturality says that these components commute with reindexing along every relevant morphism. A familiar consequence is that the Yoneda embedding is fully faithful: natural maps between representable interaction profiles correspond exactly to morphisms between their representing objects. [Reddy, Categories and Functors, §3.3, Lemmas 15–17][yoneda].

That theorem is not “a thing equals any one of its shadows.” It concerns the complete, coherent family of interactions and their naturality. A partial test suite, a poetic token or one local Spider encounter does not automatically satisfy those hypotheses.

The HFO lineage offers a specific interpretation:

> Characterize an actor/capability by the complete typed interactions it supports at the ABI and the observable receipts those interactions produce, rather than by hidden implementation identity.

The source labels this a **Yoneda-inspired ABI law**, an architectural analogy. A provider, model or session may change while a declared interface class is preserved; observational equivalence remains bounded to the interactions and acceptance conditions actually examined. [Gen137 holonarchy, lines 169–181][abi].
